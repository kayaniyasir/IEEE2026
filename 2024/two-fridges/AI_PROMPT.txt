Problem
Each substance stays safe within an inclusive integer temperature interval [a,b]. Select two integer refrigerator temperatures such that every interval contains at least one chosen temperature. Among solutions, choose the lexicographically smallest ordered pair.

Input
Integer N, then N lines with a and b.

Output
T1 T2 with T1 <= T2, or -1 when no valid pair exists.

Constraints and fidelity
0<=N<=100; -100<=a<=b<=100; choose both temperatures within [-100,100].

Sample input

2
10 12
20 24


Sample output

10 20

HOW TO SOLVE THE QUESTION
1. Every substance i has an allowed inclusive temperature interval [a_i,b_i]. Choose x<=y in the allowed range [-100,100].

2. Enumerate x from -100 to 100. For each x, enumerate y from x to 100, guaranteeing increasing lexicographic pair order.

3. For each candidate pair, scan all intervals. The interval is covered when (a_i<=x<=b_i) OR (a_i<=y<=b_i).

4. If a pair covers ALL intervals, print it immediately: it is the lexicographically smallest valid choice.

5. After exhausting every candidate, output -1.

WHY THE ALGORITHM IS CORRECT
There are 201 integer temperatures and only 20,301 sorted pairs. Exhaustive checking is small, robust, and gives the optimal tie choice automatically.

SAMPLE WALKTHROUGH
Input intervals [10,12] and [20,24]. No single temperature covers both, so first choose the smallest permitted temperature 10 and second 20; output 10 20.

EXPECTED WORST-CASE COMPLEXITY
O(201²*N) time and O(N) space; bounded loops make this feasible for N<=100.
